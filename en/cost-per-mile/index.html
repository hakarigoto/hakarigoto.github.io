Why the per-mile number matters

Two drivers can own the identical car with identical bills and have completely different costs per mile. The one driving 15,000 miles a year spreads the fixed costs thin; the one driving 3,000 miles pays the same bills for a car that mostly sits. The per-mile figure is what tells you whether your car is earning its keep — and it is the number you need for any honest comparison with rideshare or transit.

The cost everyone forgets

Depreciation never shows up on a bill, which is why it gets left out of most mental math. But it is real money: the difference between what your car was worth when you got it and what it will be worth when you let it go. For many cars it is the single largest ownership cost. You do not need a formula — used-car listings for your model and year, now and for three-year-older examples, give you a perfectly usable estimate.

Common questions

How do I estimate depreciation without a formula?

Take what you could sell the car for today, subtract what you expect it to be worth in three years, and divide by three. Listings for your model and year on any major used-car site give you both numbers. It is an estimate, but far better than leaving depreciation out.

Should I include my loan payment?

If you are still paying off the car, include the payments — they are money the car costs you every month. Just avoid double-counting: the loan pays for the car's value, which overlaps with depreciation. Either count payments and skip depreciation, or count depreciation and skip the principal part of your payments.

Why is my cost per mile so high?

Usually because you drive less than you think. Fixed costs stay the same no matter how little you drive, so at low mileage every mile carries a large share of them. That is exactly the situation where comparing against rideshare is worth doing.

What costs does this calculator leave out?

Tolls, parking tickets, car washes, accessories, and the value of your time. It also does not model loan interest separately — include it in your fixed costs if you want it counted.

How the calculation works
  • Total annual cost: insurance + registration + maintenance + depreciation (or payments) + (monthly parking × 12) + (miles ÷ MPG × gas price)
  • Cost per mile: total annual cost ÷ miles per year
  • Fixed share: everything except fuel, shown so you can see how much of your cost does not change with driving
  • Not included: tolls, tickets, washes, accessories, loan interest as a separate line, and the value of your time
  • Default values are editable starting points, not recommendations. Replace them with your own figures.
