What to Do With a Car That No Longer Runs

A dead or badly damaged car still has value — in its metal, its parts, or as someone else's project. It also still has obligations: it's registered to you until the paperwork says otherwise. This page compares the four realistic exits and walks through the title and DMV steps that keep you out of trouble. We don't rank services or name companies here.

Updated: August 2026
The short version
  • Junk-car buyers pay cash and tow it away — quotes vary a lot, so get 2–3

  • Parting out pays the most per hour of anyone's time — but only if that someone is you, with tools and space

Each option in detail

Sell to a junk-car buyer

Companies that buy non-running cars quote a price from your description, then tow the car and pay you. It's the default exit for most people: fast, no repairs, no strangers test-driving anything.

  • Get 2–3 quotes. Offers for the same car genuinely differ, and one call each is cheap insurance
  • Describe the car honestly (damage, missing parts, whether it rolls) — surprises at pickup are what kill offers
  • Confirm who handles the title and how you get paid before the tow truck arrives; never pay an upfront fee to sell

If the car actually runs and drives, you may leave money on the table here — check the sell / trade-in / instant-offer comparison first.

Donate it

Many charities run vehicle-donation programs and will tow a non-runner. The honest framing: donate because you support the cause. A tax deduction exists but only helps if you itemize deductions, and IRS rules generally tie the amount to what the charity actually sells the car for, with acknowledgment paperwork.

  • Verify the organization is a registered charity, and ask how the title transfer is handled
  • Keep the written acknowledgment; see IRS guidance on vehicle donations for the current rules

Part it out

Individual parts — wheels, lights, electronics, body panels — often sell for more in total than any whole-car offer. This is the highest-return path if you have the tools, the space, and the patience for listings and shipping.

  • Budget honest time: weeks to months, plus disposing of the stripped shell (usually a scrap yard) at the end
  • The classic failure mode is the half-parted car that sits for a year — set a deadline for calling a junk buyer for the rest

Take it to a scrap yard yourself

Yards pay by weight at current scrap prices. Worth comparing when junk-buyer quotes seem low and you can get the car there (or the yard tows for a fee). Call ahead — yards have their own requirements for title and ID, and some drain fluids differently.

The paperwork that protects you

The money part of a junk car is small; the liability part is not. The goal is simple: after the car leaves, nothing about it should trace back to you. The exact steps vary by state, so use your state DMV's website as the authoritative checklist. The usual shape:

  • Sign over the title to the buyer, yard or charity. Lost the title? Your state DMV has a duplicate-title process — start it before scheduling a pickup
  • Plates: many states have you remove and return or transfer them; some don't. Check your state's rule
  • Notify the DMV of the transfer where your state provides it (often called a release of liability or notice of transfer) — this is the step that ends tickets and tolls in your name
  • Cancel the insurance once the car is gone — after the transfer, not before
  • Remove personal items and the garage door opener, and clear phone pairings from the infotainment
Don't abandon it. An abandoned vehicle can mean fines, towing charges and continued liability, because the car is still registered to you. Even a worthless car should exit through a documented transfer.

Common questions

How much is a junk car worth?

It depends on weight, scrap prices, parts demand and whether it rolls. Quotes genuinely differ between buyers — getting 2–3 quotes is the single most effective move.

Is donating worth it for the tax deduction?

Only if you itemize, and IRS rules generally limit the deduction to what the charity sells the car for. Donate for the cause; treat the deduction as a bonus.

Can I just abandon the car?

No — fines, towing charges and continued liability, since it's still registered to you. Exit through a documented transfer.

What paperwork do I need?

Title signed over, plates handled per your state's rule, DMV notified of the transfer where provided, insurance cancelled after. Your state DMV's site is the authoritative checklist.
